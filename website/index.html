Building a black hole simulator.

A physical representation of spinning black holes.

The physics

Spinning black holes are likely to make up most of the astrophysical black holes in the universe. These spinning black holes are represented by the Kerr metric, discovered by Roy Kerr in 1963. This simulation uses The Kerr metric in Kerr-Schild coordinates in natural units:

This equation represents how the worldline (path through spacetime) of matter or light is affected by spinning mass.

Photon initialization and integration

The following simulation is composed by initializing every stage pixel as a photon from a ZAMO (zero angular momentum observer) and taking a locally minkowski, or flat, spacetime to seed the initial conditions of an rk4 integrator. the integrand is taken as a state 8 - vector comprised of the 4-velocity and the covariant 4-momentum, also known as the phase space. And colourized using the final position of the photon over the affine permater lambda.

Prograde-only orbits

Photon oribts are considered for only prograde motion. Superimposing prograde and retrograde orbits would not give visible difference on the accretion disc as the prograde orbits extend closer to the shadow than the retrograde orbits.

Accretion disc model

Disc mechanics use Bardeen, Press, Teukolsky (1973) for the inner extent of the disc and the Energy as well as angular Momentum functions. Remembering that E & L are conserved via Killing fields in Kerr spacetime. The E & L equations are used in deriving the flux - energy profile for the accretion disc using Novikov & Thorne (1973) model. The colour science is then done across the disc in a nodular approach using Wyman, Sloan & Shirley, (2013) with CIE (2004).

Redshift and Doppler beaming

Redhsifting is calculated by dividing observed frequency by emitted frequency, which also factors doppler beaming in Kerr spacetime, giving the excellent brightspots and darker spots expected of orbiting disc material.

Starfield and the Einstein ring

Background starfield is textured against the HYG database starfield using normalized escape directions of photons bypassing the black hole. This method produces the Einstein ring excellently well.
